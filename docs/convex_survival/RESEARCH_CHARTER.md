# BACQE Convex Survival Research Charter

## Purpose

The BACQE Convex Survival research platform exists to study the
decision-making behaviour of systematic execution strategies using
structured, reproducible evidence.

The platform is not intended to search blindly for profitable parameter
combinations.

Its purpose is to determine:

- why trading opportunities are accepted;
- why trading opportunities are rejected;
- which filters materially affect qualification;
- which filters are redundant;
- which combinations of conditions create or destroy opportunity;
- whether observed behaviour remains stable across time, symbols and
  market regimes;
- whether proposed strategy changes are supported by evidence.

## Research principles

### 1. Evidence before optimisation

No parameter should be changed merely because it appears intuitively
restrictive.

Changes should be supported by measured evidence from the research
dataset.

### 2. One-variable-at-a-time experimentation

Where possible, each formal experiment should modify one controlled
variable while preserving all other relevant conditions.

### 3. Immutable raw evidence

Original MQL5 CSV outputs must be preserved unchanged.

Python processing must never modify raw source files in place.

### 4. Deterministic processing

The same input file and configuration should produce the same staged,
curated and analytical outputs.

### 5. Research lineage

Every analytical output should be traceable to:

- the MQL5 engine version;
- the research run identifier;
- the source CSV;
- the symbol;
- the timeframe;
- the test date range;
- the Python engine version;
- the processing timestamp.

### 6. Failures are evidence

Rejected setups, failed filters and unsuccessful experiments are part of
the research record.

They must not be discarded merely because they do not improve strategy
performance.

### 7. Marginal influence matters

A filter that fails frequently is not necessarily important.

The platform must distinguish between:

- total filter failures;
- first vetoes;
- sole vetoes;
- multiple simultaneous vetoes;
- fully qualified observations.

### 8. No automatic live promotion

Research results must not directly authorise live trading.

Promotion to paper trading or live execution requires separate validation,
risk governance and explicit approval.

The first Python research phase will focus on:

1. ingestion;
2. schema validation;
3. data-quality auditing;
4. normalisation;
5. staged Parquet generation;
6. filter attribution analysis;
7. sole-veto analysis;
8. veto-combination analysis;
9. experiment recommendation support.

## Initial architecture

The MQL5 engine is the evidence producer.

The BACQE Python platform is the evidence validator, research processor
and analytical layer.

The two systems must remain independently testable while preserving
complete lineage between generated evidence and resulting analysis.
